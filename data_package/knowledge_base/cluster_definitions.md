# Cluster Definitions

Distributor retail clusters used in the ABC Planning product. Branded merchandise inherits the same cluster structure as the parent beverage portfolio.

## C-EAST

States: NY, NJ, PA, MD, DE, VA, CT, RI, MA, NH, VT, ME, DC, WV, OH.

Distribution centres: Bethlehem PA (primary), Albany NY (secondary), Springfield MA (secondary).

Retailer count: approximately 4,200 active outlets.

Profile: higher overall volume than C-WEST. Bourbon and whiskey skew is significant — the cluster includes the Mid-Atlantic spirits corridor. Beer programs are strong year-round with a modest summer lift. Drinkware historically over-indexes versus apparel.

## C-WEST

States: CA, OR, WA, NV, AZ, NM, CO, UT, ID, MT, WY.

Distribution centres: Stockton CA (primary), Portland OR (secondary), Phoenix AZ (secondary).

Retailer count: approximately 2,800 active outlets.

Profile: lower overall volume than C-EAST but higher per-store. Tequila and craft beer programs are notably stronger. The cluster is more responsive to promotional activity — historical lift on co-marketing windows runs 20–25% higher in C-WEST than in C-EAST.

## Why we only have two clusters for branded merchandise

The standard ABC Planning product uses four-cluster (NE / SE / MW / W) decomposition for beverages. Branded merchandise launched with a simplified two-cluster split because the volume does not yet justify finer granularity. We may revisit this in the next planning cycle if the category grows past projection.

## Specialty retailers

A small population of retailers operate outside the standard cluster planogram — typically premium on-premise establishments or specialty bottle shops. These retailers are designated **specialty** in the retailer master and follow distinct buying rules. They represent roughly 8% of outlets but 18% of merchandise revenue, so they are disproportionately important for category margin.
